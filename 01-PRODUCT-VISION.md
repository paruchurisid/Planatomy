# Product vision

## Product statement

Planatomy is a visual research tool that connects the anatomy of commercial passenger aircraft with evidence about replacement-component costs. Its central promise is to make aircraft complexity understandable without disguising uncertainty in aircraft configuration or price data.

A user should be able to answer four questions from one component panel: What am I looking at? What does it do? Which specific part identities are associated with this configuration? What price evidence exists, and how comparable is it?

## Why this is useful

Aircraft information is fragmented across visual references, technical catalogs, supplier listings, and private commercial records. A person who starts with a physical location or functional name may not know the part number needed to search a marketplace. Conversely, a part-number search may reveal offers without showing where the item sits inside the aircraft.

The product hypothesis is that a visual, evidence-backed connection between these information types reduces research effort and makes component economics easier to understand. This hypothesis needs user testing; no customer demand or willingness to pay has been validated yet.

## Initial users

| User | Starting question | Useful outcome | Initial priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aviation enthusiast or student | What is inside a passenger jet, and what is expensive? | Understand components and inspect sourced prices | Primary public experience |
| Aviation educator or technical writer | How can I explain aircraft systems visually? | Share a stable component view with context | Primary discovery audience |
| Parts researcher or procurement analyst | Where does this component fit and what offers exist? | Find exact identities and comparable evidence faster | Professional pilot audience |
| Maintenance planner | Which reference assembly is relevant to this configuration? | Navigate to authoritative source context | Research support only |
| Asset analyst | What documented replacement exposure exists in a selected subsystem? | Export a bounded, reproducible scenario | Later professional capability |

Avoid promising an operational maintenance system, an installation approval service, a certified valuation, or an automated purchasing agent. Those require additional workflows and evidence beyond the initial product.

## Differentiation

The proposed differentiator is the relationship between spatial exploration and evidence-backed component economics. Existing marketplaces are candidate partners as well as adjacent products. Planatomy should not assume that it can recreate their inventory or pricing datasets from public pages.

The defensible assets would be curated component-to-part mappings, configuration-aware anatomy, reviewed source adapters, trusted price normalization, and a usable interface. A generic scraper or a decorative aircraft model alone provides little differentiation.

## First aircraft strategy

Use one representative A320ceo reference configuration as the planning baseline. Determine the exact subtype, engine option, and supported configuration attributes during feasibility work. Do not label an asset as that aircraft unless its geometry and reference documentation support the label.

The final pilot selection should maximize legally usable anatomy assets, obtainable component references, and publishable price evidence. If a Boeing 737NG candidate scores substantially better, record the decision and change the pilot before building the detailed catalog. Supporting commercial passenger jets remains fixed; the first family remains a recommendation.

Do not begin with every Airbus and Boeing family. Each additional model introduces configuration work, geometry review, applicability relationships, and data quality obligations. Reuse generic component concepts across families while keeping family-specific occurrences and applicability separate.

## Product depth

| Level | Experience | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aircraft overview | Exterior, large systems, broad functional explanations | Licensed geometry and reviewed educational content |
| System explorer | Isolated systems and major assemblies | Correct system membership and approximate locations |
| Component explorer | Selectable units, quantity context, candidate part numbers | Reviewed mappings and applicability evidence |
| Price explorer | Offers, commercial terms, observations, price summaries | Identity-matched, publishable evidence |
| Scenario explorer | Bounded replacement-component subtotals | Resolved quantities, comparable basis, overlap controls |
| Fleet research | Multiple configurations and private evidence | Tenant controls and additional configuration data |

Launch at component and price explorer depth for a limited curated subset. The visual scene can contain additional unpriced educational components if the distinction is clear.

## MVP scope

Proposed alpha targets are one reference configuration, 8–12 navigable system groups, 75–150 component concepts, 50–100 selectable occurrences, and 20–40 exact part identities with usable price observations. A part identity may serve multiple occurrences. A selectable occurrence may remain unpriced.

Public beta adds a reviewed source-refresh workflow, evidence inspection, cost overlays, and a limited component basket. The basket produces a selected-component subtotal only. Price coverage targets should be reduced or the pilot revised if source rights cannot support them; fabricated values are never a substitute for coverage.

Include search by human name and part number, system filtering, hide/isolate, exploded views, exact configuration labeling, price-condition filters, source dates, and a usable text fallback. Include an internal review interface from the first real data import.

## Deferred capabilities

Individual tail-number configuration histories, full illustrated parts catalogs, serialized life tracking, transaction settlement, automated requests for quotes, fleet maintenance predictions, comprehensive engine teardown pricing, private customer quote ingestion, and aircraft-wide part-out valuation are later capabilities. Each requires a new evidence and product gate.

AI-generated component explanations may be drafted for review later. AI should not invent hidden geometry, installed quantities, part compatibility, or market prices.

## Business hypotheses

Start with public educational discovery and a small professional research pilot. Potential paid capabilities include licensed historical evidence, saved comparison scenarios, team annotations, permitted exports, and private organization catalogs. Sponsored placement must be visibly separate from evidence ranking. Any referral relationship must be disclosed near the outbound offer.

Monetization depends on redistribution rights and evidence utility. A license that allows internal use may prohibit resale, public display, or derived analytics. Evaluate rights before including a dataset in a paid plan. Revenue projections are deliberately absent until discovery interviews and provider quotes establish a basis.

## Success measures

The main usage measure is an evidence-backed component investigation: a visitor selects a component, reaches an applicable part identity or sees why none is available, and inspects the supporting commercial evidence.

Track the proportion of users who can find a named component in two minutes, correctly distinguish an asking price from an estimate, and identify when a displayed value is stale. For professional pilots, compare time to a usable evidence set against their current research process.

Track data success separately: share of mapped pilot occurrences, share of curated identities with fresh usable evidence, independent seller coverage, extraction error rate, and human-review effort per published identity. Do not optimize a raw count of scraped pages as a product success metric.

Initial usability targets are 80% completion of the component-finding task and 90% correct interpretation of missing-price and exchange-price states across at least 10 representative participants. These are proposed launch gates, not statistical claims about the broader market.

## Long-term direction

Expand from one aircraft to an aircraft-family atlas, then to configuration comparisons and organization-specific research. The long-term experience could connect a visual aircraft to a history of component observations and replacement scenarios. Expansion should follow evidence availability, not just the availability of attractive exterior models.
